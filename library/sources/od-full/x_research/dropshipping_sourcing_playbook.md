# Dropshipping Sourcing Playbook — September 2026

## The one thing that reframes everything

The four platforms you listed (AliExpress, Alibaba, CJ, Zendrop) are all the *same tier*: beginner-friendly catalogs you browse and click. Nobody scaling past a few hundred orders a day is still sourcing that way. The real hierarchy looks like this:

| Tier | What it is | When you move here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Catalog apps | AliExpress/DSers, CJ, Zendrop, Spocket | 0–10 orders/day — testing |
| 2. Fulfillment agents | HyperSKU, EPROLO, Sourcinbox, Wiio, NicheDropshipping | 10+ orders/day — the real unlock |
| 3. Source marketplaces | 1688, Taobao, Yiwugo, Made-in-China, Global Sources | You know your winner and want factory price |
| 4. Private agent / direct factory | WeChat agent, your own QC, your own 3PL | 100+ orders/day, one hero product |
| 5. Domestic stock + 3PL | ShipBob, ShipMonk, ShipCalm | You're a brand, not a dropshipper |

Most stores plateau because they never leave Tier 1. Going from AliExpress to a real agent typically cuts COGS 20–40% and shipping time in half, on the exact same product.

---

## Tier 1 — Testing (keep these, but don't live here)

**AliExpress + DSers** — DSers is the official Oberlo replacement and AliExpress's own partner app. Free tier is fine. Use it purely to validate demand. Never scale on it: prices float, suppliers vanish, no QC, generic packaging.

**CJ Dropshipping** — the closest thing to "AliExpress but built for dropshippers." Free, no subscription, huge catalog, US/EU warehouses, and it's the only Tier 1 platform that assigns you a human agent. Genuinely the strongest of your four. Downside: quoted processing times slip when a product goes viral.

**Zendrop** — better UX, US warehouse options, custom packaging and branded inserts earlier than most. Costs a monthly subscription and its product prices are usually higher than CJ for the same SKU. Good for a clean start, bad for margin.

**Alibaba** — this is *wholesale/bulk*, not dropshipping. Use it for private-label runs and MOQ orders, not order-by-order fulfillment. Trade Assurance is real protection — use it.

**Verdict on your four:** CJ is the best of them. Zendrop is a convenience tax. Alibaba is a different tool for a different job. AliExpress is a research tool.

---

## Tier 2 — Fulfillment agents (this is what you're actually missing)

These are China-based teams that source from 1688/factories, do quality control, add your branding, and ship — with a real human on WhatsApp/WeChat. Nearly all are free to join; you pay per order.

**HyperSKU** — the most commonly recommended step-up. Pay-as-you-go, no subscription, no MOQ, strong branding/custom packaging, US warehouse routes, built for 10+ orders/day. Best default choice.

**EPROLO** — completely free, no MOQ, warehouses in US/UK/EU/China, branding from your first sale. Best value if you're margin-tight and want to start building a brand identity immediately.

**Sourcinbox** — dedicated agent per account, flexible MOQ, strong Shopify sync. Well-reviewed on the Shopify App Store.

**Wiio** — clean and reliable for consistent daily volume; does custom product photos/videos, inserts, labels, and inspects goods before shipping.

**NicheDropshipping** — Yiwu + Shenzhen + US facilities, one-on-one sourcing, strongest of this group for private-label and branded builds. No subscription.

**Dropshipman** — agent service plus branding with 24/7 support; commonly used by TikTok Shop sellers.

**Sup Dropshipping** — free tier, then $19.90/mo Pro and $29.90/mo Premium. Cheap entry.

> Practical note: most quality agents want ~10 orders/day before they'll give you real attention and real pricing. Some accept 1 unit. Sign up with 2–3 in parallel, send the same product link to each, and compare the quotes. The spread will surprise you.

## Tier 3 — Where the products actually come from

**1688.com** — Alibaba's Chinese domestic wholesale site. Roughly **30–70% cheaper than Taobao**, which is itself **30–80% cheaper than AliExpress** on the same goods. This is the price your agent is paying. Chinese-only, requires an agent or forwarder to buy. Use it to sanity-check whether your agent's quote is fair.

**Taobao** — best for *discovery*. Chinese trends surface here months before they hit Western dropshipping stores. Access via an agent (Fishgoo, Superbuy, Wegobuy, Pandabuy-style services).

**Yiwugo / Yiwu Market** — the world's small-commodities hub. If your product is a gadget, home item, or seasonal decor, it is physically sold in Yiwu. Agents based in Yiwu get the best pricing on this category.

**Made-in-China.com & Global Sources** — better verified-manufacturer databases than Alibaba for anything technical or certified (electronics, appliances). Global Sources vets harder.

**DHgate** — small-MOQ wholesale, useful middle ground, but quality is a lottery. Test heavily.

## Tier 4 — US / EU domestic suppliers (fast shipping)

Worth much more in 2026 than in previous years because of the tariff situation below.

**Spocket** — the strongest curated US/EU supplier network, 3–7 day delivery. Subscription-based and product costs are higher, but the delivery speed converts better and kills chargebacks.

**Syncee** — similar model, large curated supplier marketplace, good EU coverage.

**Modalyst** — strong on apparel and recognizable brands, US/EU suppliers.

**TopDawg** — US-based, broad catalog, well-regarded for genuine domestic fulfillment.

**Trendsi** — apparel-specific, US warehouses, very popular with fashion stores.

**Doba / Wholesale2B / Inventory Source** — aggregator-style directories connecting to hundreds of US suppliers with multi-channel sync (Shopify, Amazon, eBay). Functional but dated; margins are usually thin.

**Faire** — not a dropshipping tool, but the best place to find real US/EU independent brands to wholesale. Worth knowing if you want a legitimate branded store.

**SaleHoo** — a vetted supplier *directory*, not a fulfiller. Useful one-time purchase for finding domestic partners nobody else has found.

## Print on Demand

**Printful** — best production quality and consistency, in-house printing, best for US-focused premium branded merch. Highest base cost (~$11.69 for a tee).

**Printify** — cheapest (~$10.36 tee), largest catalog (800+ items), global print network. Quality varies by print partner — you must test and lock in a specific partner.

**Gelato** — best if you sell internationally; prints locally in ~30 countries, so no cross-border shipping or duties. ~$9.80–11.50 depending on region.

**SPOD** — fastest US turnaround. **Gooten** — solid mid-size option.

## Tier 5 — When you become a brand

Once one product is doing real volume, stop dropshipping it. Bulk-order via Alibaba/1688, ship by sea, and hold stock in a US 3PL: **ShipBob** (best all-round), **ShipMonk**, **ShipCalm** (TikTok Shop integration, LA + Indianapolis), **ShipBots** (Long Beach, TikTok-first). Delivery drops to 2–3 days and COGS drops again.

## The 2026 reality check that changes the math

**The $800 de minimis exemption is dead.** Timeline: it ended for China/Hong Kong on 2 May 2025, for all other countries on 29 Aug 2025, and CBP made the suspension **indefinite by regulation effective 24 June 2026**. It also ends by statute in 2027, so it is not coming back.

What this means for you concretely:

1. Every single parcel entering the US now gets assessed duties. Sub-$800 China parcels have faced rates around 54%, or a flat per-item fee under the postal route. **Your landed cost is no longer your supplier's quoted price.**
2. Direct-from-China single-parcel dropshipping has structurally worse economics than it did 18 months ago. The 30–70% price gap between 1688 and AliExpress is now the buffer that keeps you profitable — which is exactly why moving to a Tier 2 agent is no longer optional.
3. Routing through a **US warehouse** (CJ, HyperSKU, EPROLO, Zendrop, or a 3PL) means duty is paid once on a bulk import rather than per parcel. This is usually cheaper *and* faster now.
4. Shifting sourcing to Vietnam/India/Mexico does **not** dodge this — the suspension covers all countries.
5. Price your products with duty modeled in, and set customer-facing delivery estimates honestly. Customs delays are the #1 new source of chargebacks.

*Note: some vendor blogs still claim sub-$800 parcels are exempt. They are out of date. Trust the Federal Register and CBP over supplier marketing.*

## How to vet an agent before you commit

1. Send the same product URL to 3–4 agents. Compare unit price, shipping cost, and quoted delivery — the spread is often 40%+.
2. Order **one sample to yourself** before routing a single customer order. Non-negotiable.
3. Ask directly: who pays for a lost parcel, a broken item, a wrong variant? Get the answer in writing.
4. Ask for their 1688 source link. Good agents share it. Agents who refuse are hiding a markup.
5. Check whether they can do custom packaging/inserts at your current volume, not at some future volume.
6. Test their response time on a weekend before you depend on them.

**Red flags:** upfront "membership" fees to access pricing, refusal to send samples, no named human contact, pressure to prepay large balances, prices that shift after you commit.

---

## Recommended stack

- **Testing a new product:** AliExpress/DSers or CJ — no commitment, just validate.
- **Product hits 10+ orders/day:** move it to HyperSKU or EPROLO immediately. This is the single highest-ROI action available to you.
- **US-speed offer or TikTok Shop:** Spocket or Trendsi, or an agent's US warehouse.
- **Price-checking your agent:** 1688 via image search.
- **Product hits 100+ orders/day:** bulk order + ShipBob.
