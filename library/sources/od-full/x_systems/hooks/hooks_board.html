How to use this board

  1. Ask for a hook by its number. “Do hook 08 for video 5 of PRODUCT X” is the whole instruction. Numbers are stable and never reused — a new hook takes the next number, it never replaces an old one.
  2. The card is the complete brief. Open Prompt & notes and you get the verbatim image prompt, the verbatim clip prompt, the exact model and settings, the job ids where they were recorded, and the references in the order they were attached. Nothing else is needed to re-run the shot.
  3. Copy the prompt, do not rewrite it. Use the Copy button, then change only the nouns that physically differ: the product, the box, the room, the person. Everything describing motion, camera, pacing, framing and the ban lists stays byte-identical. Two prompts that mean the same thing to a human produce different videos — rewording is the bug, and it is the most common way these ports fail.
  4. Follow the Swap for a new product list. It names, per hook, which clauses are the product and which are load-bearing. That list is the difference between a port that works first take and four wasted generations.
  5. Reference order matters. Where a card lists two references, the first owns the geometry and the second owns the look. Swapping them changes the output.
  6. Log every reuse. Each card ends with a Reuses table. After a port, add one row: date, product, video and shot, what was changed, whether it worked, and the one-line lesson. That table is how this board gets better — a hook with four green rows is a hook you can trust on a cold product.
  7. Generate once, then review. One take per shot, then stop. Never self-review and re-roll.

What is true across all 14

  1. A hook is the C01 shot. Every entry is the opening shot of its video — the 4 to 5 seconds that decide whether the rest gets watched. No B-roll on this board.
  2. Two prompts per hook, not one. A still is generated first, then animated. The image prompt owns geometry, wardrobe, set and product; the clip prompt owns motion, timing and camera only. If a note is about motion, timing or a count, edit the clip prompt and re-roll on the same starting frame — do not regenerate the still.
  3. Anything that has to move must already exist in the starting frame. A prop that appears only in the clip prompt pops in and reads fake.
  4. Beats need explicit time ranges. 0.0-1.5s — … animates; “then she turns” does not. A beat that keeps going missing is usually a duration problem, not a wording problem — hook 08 lost its third count at 4 s and got it back at 5 s, first try.
  5. A video reference imports its own audio and has to be at least 2 s long. Attach a silenced copy and say in the prompt that it is silent. It imports content too, not just motion — hook 13 blended two clips together and had to be re-run with no video reference at all.
  6. No text in frame, ever. Every prompt ends with the ban. Hook copy is burned in CapCut afterwards.
  7. Three archive gaps exist and are marked in red on the cards. Hooks 03 and 10 have no archived image prompt; hook 11 never had one of its own. A missing prompt is never reconstructed from the picture or from a neighbouring take.

Across all four videos FRAMING AND ANGLE, THE KNOT AND THE PLUG, THE CONNECTION, THE TREE IS NOT SWITCHED ON, LIGHTING AND QUALITY and the caption band are the same bytes; only THE ROOM, THE TREE, THE MOTHER, THE CHILDREN and one COLOUR clause ever changed. All four frames came back with the same camera, the same kneeling pose and the same wire. Rewording the geometry to “make it different” is how this drive has lost framings before.

Worked — @Image 1 is always the master frame

v12, v17 and v22 all attached v9_c01_g8.png as @Image 1, and all three frames and all three clips passed first take. Chaining off the newest port instead would copy that port’s drift.

Fixed on the master — the reel’s continuity bug

In the owner’s reel the lights she plugs in are not connected to the tree. THE CONNECTION block (one unbroken wire from plug → knot → floor → tree) plus THE TREE IS NOT SWITCHED ON fixed it; the tree must be dark in the frame or the fire has no cause. Two options were generated (g7, g8); the owner picked g8.

Worked — the camera IS the phone

“Held iPhone” written into a POV prompt renders a phone into the bottom of the frame. The opening paragraph declares the camera to BE the phone and bans phone, screen, hand, fingers, thumb, arm, sleeve. No phone appeared in any of the four clips.

Failed, then fixed — the camera ran down the hallway (v9 clip g5)

g5 said the camera retreats; it walked backwards the whole hall. The owner: “The person filming must make just a few steps back not run the entire hall. In rest is good.” The fix was not a softer adjective: the retreat got an end time (“finished by about 1.6s”), the rest of the clip got a stated camera state (“STANDING STILL … not moving backwards at all”), and the failure itself went into BANNED. A camera move needs a stop time the same way a beat needs a start time.

Known and accepted — the fire is late

The prompt fuses plug, bulbs, flame, shout and flinch into one instant at 0.8 s; every take still renders plug → flash → fire. Measured: v9 g6 bulbs ≈1.5 s, fire ≈1.8 s; the ports light up by ≈1.7 s and burn by ≈2.0 s. The owner approved all four this way and asked for g6 to be kept as is. Do not change the timing on a port. The written-up fix, untested: move the instant to 0.4 s and delete “flashes on for a split second” — only if the owner asks.

Minor, accepted

v12: a few small bright points on the “unlit” tree (flagged, approved). v17: the REALISM room list kept stale nouns from v9 (“same navy sofa with the mustard blanket”) — the clip passed, but it is the swap error to avoid. The tape roll in her left hand arrived in v17 (reason not archived) and was kept in v22; v9 and v12 have none and passed too. Default: keep it, since you copy v22. To drop it, delete the tape clause in THE MOTHER, the THE TAPE ROLL paragraph and PROP CONTINUITY — nothing else.
