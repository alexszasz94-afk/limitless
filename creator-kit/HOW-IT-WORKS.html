Robe Studio · the complete guide

One reel in, a finished robe video out.

Robe Studio makes ready-to-post TikTok and Reels videos for our heated hooded robes. You send it a reel that is working for someone else, or an idea of your own. It rebuilds the video with our robe, a new woman and a new room, checks every frame, edits it with music, and hands you the MP4 plus the exact text to add in CapCut.

You never edit anything. No video editor, no files, no code, no settings. You talk to Claude in normal sentences, like texting a friend who edits videos for you. If you want something different, you just say it.

Picking references

Which reels work

Almost any short reel with one clear moment can be rebuilt. These kinds were all tested:

  • At home: countdowns ("3, 2, 1…"), unrolling or dropping the robe, try-ons, a mom/daughter surprise. Our woman, in our room — the same room as the robe part.
  • In a store (Target, H&M, a mall): the store, its signs and logos stay exactly as in the reel; only the woman and the product change (a shelf of our boxes instead of theirs).
  • In a car or at a drive-thru (McDonald's, Starbucks): their place stays; the tray, bag or box becomes ours.
  • POV shots (we only see hands or legs): Claude still uses the woman's photo so the hands and skin match.
  • Box, drawer and tray reveals: each robe has its own official box — Snoopy the camel HEATPUP box, Princess the royal-blue "Princess · Heated Robe" box — so the logo is always spelled right.

Best references: 10–20 s, lots of views, a strong first 3 seconds, one or two people, a clear moment (a reveal, a drop, a surprise).

Deep dive · hooks

How a reel is copied

When you send a link, Claude doesn't just "get inspired". It rebuilds the reel's hook so it moves and sounds like the original, but with our robe and different people.

  1. Downloads the reel and studies it frame by frame. It writes down every action with its second and speed, for example "0–2.9 s she holds the rolled robe still; at 2.9 s she lets it unroll".
  2. Makes a list of every object and its job. Their product becomes our robe, and it must clearly look like a robe (hood, sleeves, full length), never a cape or a blanket. Their packaging becomes our box with our brand. Their themed props (a character pillow, a blanket) become the same kind of object with our print.
  3. Removes their caption, cleanly. First Claude checks if the camera stands still. If it does, the caption is covered with a clean photo of the same spot (never blur — blur shows up as a smudge). If the camera moves, the video tool is simply told to remove the caption.
  4. Keeps their place when it is not a home. Store signs and logos stay exactly as in the original. At home, the place becomes our room.
  5. Changes the people. Our woman replaces theirs, and anyone else gets a clearly different face, so it is a remake, never a copy. The same outfit (written down once) is used in the hook and in the robe part.
  6. Generates the new hook from their real footage. The motion, speed and seconds stay identical. Their clip is cut exactly where their hook ends (the last picture held still, never a piece of the next scene).
  7. Puts the big moment on the original's second. The video tool sometimes makes the reveal a little early. Claude then lengthens the calm moment just before it, invisibly, so the reveal lands on the same second as in the original. The hook ends exactly where theirs ends.
  8. Compares ours with theirs, side by side. Ten pictures per second. Anything wrong (a wrong object, a glitch, a missing action) is regenerated.

On-screen text: Claude suggests the reference's own text, word for word with the same emojis, changing only their product or character name to ours (for example "Do NOT let a Tiana fan see this" → "Do NOT let a Princess fan see this"). You type it in CapCut at the top of the screen.

Tip: start frame first looks more pro. For many hooks it is better to first make one realistic photo of the first moment, look at it, fix it until you like it, and only then animate it. Say start frame first when you send a reel. It is also the way to go when the original waits a long time before its big moment.

Your own hooks

When you describe an idea, Claude creates a realistic photo of the first moment with our woman in her room, and shows it to you. You say OK or what to change, then how it should move and whether someone says something. Claude animates it, checks it and builds the rest of the video around it.

Deep dive · sound

Sound and music

The hook's sound

The hook's sound is made together with the picture, in the same generation, so voices are on the lips and every sound is on its action. Before that, the original's sound is studied in three parts:

  1. Who says what. Higgsfield's video analysis listens to the reel and writes down who speaks and the exact words. If nobody speaks (only a song), our hook gets no voice.
  2. Voice, actions, background. Claude separates the voice from the rest and measures the second every word starts ("three" at 0.15 s, "two" at 0.97 s…), lists the sounds of the actions (a swoosh, a thud, footsteps) and the room sound underneath.
  3. All of that goes into the instructions: the exact words on their seconds, each action sound on its moment, a quiet continuous room sound, and absolutely no music (our song is added in the edit).
  4. Checked by Claude. Claude compares the words and their seconds with the original, looks at a picture of the sound to make sure no music slipped in, and fills any dead-silent gap with the hook's own quiet room sound. The measuring tools are only advice — a hook is redone only for a real problem (wrong or missing words, music, a word clearly late).

Music

Christmas songs shared by both robes, a different one each video and never the same as the last two: All I Want for Christmas Is You, It's Beginning to Look a Lot Like Christmas, Last Christmas, Let It Go. The song plays softly under the hook (the voice stays on top) and its big moment — the drop — lands exactly on the cut from the hook to the robe part, then plays full to the end. The loudness is evened out, so the video never gets quieter after the hook.

Want a different song? Drag the MP3 into the chat and say "use this song".

Deep dive · faces and places

Women and rooms

Every video uses a new woman and a new room, so your account never shows the same girl twice in a row.

Women

Real, sharp photos of real women: one woman, face clearly visible, eyes open, light skin (so she matches the hands in the ready-made connector and remote close-ups). Claude always takes the sharpest photo that hasn't been used yet, and throws out blurry ones by itself. When fewer than 5 are left, Claude searches Pinterest and keeps only real, sharp photos.

Rooms

The kit comes with 40 rooms in 4K, made by Alex, each with a sofa in a logical place (the cozy ending is filmed on that sofa). Claude uses them in order. Alex can send more rooms as a zip: drag it into the chat and say "add these rooms".

  • Sofa needed: a room without a visible sofa is skipped (the woman is kept).
  • Always in the foreground: the woman is never behind a table or a plant; if a coffee table is in the way, Claude first makes a version of the room without it.
  • One place per video: the same room for the hook (when it happens at home) and the robe part.
  • Outfit under the robe: a different one each video (Christmas pajamas, a cozy set, a college sweatshirt), always long pants, the same in hook and robe part.
Deep dive · showing the robe

The product part

  • B-roll: made from our own finished, approved robe videos (version 4 or 5, taking turns), rebuilt with the new woman, the new room and the robe copied exactly from its product photos. The instructions are frozen word for word, because they worked.
  • Version 4 detail: the moment the robe swings open is shortened by 0.3 s in the edit, so it opens smoothly.
  • Connector and remote: the same ready-made close-ups on the robe's fabric in every video. Never generated, never wrong.
  • Boxes: Snoopy's camel HEATPUP box and Princess's royal-blue "Princess" box, made once and reused, so the brand is always spelled right.
  • Feet: a toe or a sock showing for a moment while she steps into the foot pockets is fine and is not redone (Alex: "how it is now is ok").
  • Save: after every video Claude offers 2 more hooks on the same B-roll — those videos only need a new hook.
  • Cozy ending: the classic wide shot on the sofa, or a new 5-second shot of whatever you ask for: reading, hot cocoa, wrapping gifts, a movie under the hood.
Quality

How mistakes are caught

AI video sometimes makes mistakes: a hand with six fingers, a robe that melts, a person who changes. Robe Studio looks for them before you ever see the video.

CheckWhat it catches
Every photo, looked atWrong face, wrong robe, extra fingers, misspelled box, captions where there shouldn't be any
Hook vs originalActions too early, too late, too fast; a different length; wrong objects
Object listAn object with the wrong job: a blanket turned into our robe, a box without our brand, our robe looking like a cape
SoundWrong or missing words, words off their second, music that slipped in, dead-silent gaps
B-roll, shot by shotThe same woman (face and hair) in every shot, the right room and its real sofa, sitting on the sofa (not standing) in the cozy shot, no glitches
Final videoNo burned-in text, no flash frame between parts, music drop on the cut, never quieter after the hook, the right order

Anything that fails is redone with the problem written into the instructions. If something still isn't perfect, Claude tells you exactly what and why.

Your account stays fresh

Staying fresh

Robe Studio remembers what each video used: hook, woman, room, outfit, robe version, music, on-screen text and cozy ending. The next video is always different from the last three, so followers never feel they're watching the same thing again.

Honest limits

How far it can go

Works great

  • Reels where one or two people do something with an object: reveals, countdowns, unboxings, try-ons, store shelves, drive-thru surprises.
  • Hooks with spoken lines, matched on the second.
  • Your own ideas: any scene you can describe in a sentence or two.

Can still go wrong

  • The video tool sometimes adds music to a hook or leaves silent gaps — Claude fixes it or redoes it once.
  • The big moment can come early — fixed in the edit, but a very long wait before it is better made with "start frame first".
  • Clothes can change slightly between the hook and the robe part (a pattern a little different).
  • Crowds, very fast cuts every half second, tiny text on a phone screen.
  • A hook may need a second try now and then; that is normal.

Claude can't post for you or promise a video goes viral. The ideas you pick make the difference. New robes and improvements arrive through Alex's updates.

For Alex: a new video with a new B-roll uses about 60–90 Higgsfield credits including one redo; a video on an existing B-roll only the hook (about 20). Creators never see credit numbers — Claude answers them in videos.

Where you make the difference

Your part

The technical work is handled. Your energy goes into ideas:

  • Find reels that already work: short (10–20 s), lots of views, a strong first 3 seconds.
  • Invent your own hooks: try one of your own for every two or three copied reels.
  • Add the suggested text in CapCut before posting (top of the screen, the whole video).
  • Make 2 more hooks on each B-roll when Claude offers. Quicker and cheaper.
  • Change the cozy ending now and then.
  • Post consistently, and notice which hooks get the most views. Do more of those.
Help

If something goes wrong

  • Claude says Higgsfield isn't connected: open claude.ai/customize/connectors, connect Higgsfield again, start a new chat.
  • A link can't be downloaded: save the video on your phone or computer and drop it into the chat.
  • Claude says a download was blocked: check the Network access step in the setup guide, then start a new chat.
  • Credits ran out: top up on higgsfield.ai.
  • Anything else: ask what went wrong? and Claude explains and fixes it. Still stuck? Write to Alex.
